# 07 — Floors, World & Lore

---

## 1. The Premise

Arkham, Massachusetts, 1928. A door in the sub-cellar of the Miskatonic University library has been ajar since the witch trials. It does not lead down into the earth; it leads *down* in some other sense, and the further you go the less the geography of Massachusetts has to do with it.

You are a member of the **Order of the Nine Angles of Arkham** — not a hero, not an investigator, and not, quite, a victim. You have chosen this. Your motive is character-specific and revealed through Codex fragments, never cutscenes.

**What you want:** to reach the bottom and wake the thing that dreams there, or to reach the bottom and make sure it never wakes. The game does not decide this for you until the very end. See §5.

## 3. Floor Detail

### Floor 1 — Arkham Undercroft

**Look:** Wet red brick, hand-cut stone from 1690, tallow candles as the only light source. Warm amber pools in a deep near-black. Wooden shelving, salt lines on thresholds, a lot of rats.

**Mood:** *Underexplained.* This still looks like a place a person built. That's the horror — someone put this here on purpose.

**Mechanics introduced:** doors seal on room entry; Blink Step; Recitation; the Sanity ring; the first shop; the first sigil.

**Signature rooms:**
- *The Salt Threshold* — a connector where salt lines on the floor block enemies but not you. Teaches environmental reading.
- *The Chandler's Room* — pitch dark except for candles you can shoot out (or light). Teaches that light is a resource.
- *The Long Table* — a single 40×9 room with flippable tables. The tutorial for cover.

**Lore beat:** graffiti in an old hand, in English, gradually becoming not-English. The Codex translates it a fragment per run.

---

### Floor 2 — The Drowned Wharfs of Innsmouth

**Look:** Grey-green, rotted pilings, gambrel roofs at wrong angles, gold everywhere — Innsmouth gold, the refinery, the reef offshore. Perpetual dusk. Rain.

**Mood:** *Watched.* Everything here is a face if you look at it long enough.

**Mechanic — the Tide:** water level oscillates on a 20s cycle across the whole floor, visible as a rising tide line. Deep water: player move speed ×0.7, Deep One speed ×2.0, lightning damage +40% to Drenched targets. **The tide is synchronised across the floor**, so it is predictable and can be planned around — it's a rhythm layer over the combat rhythm, not a random hazard.

**Signature rooms:**
- *The Refinery* — molten gold hazard, and the floor's richest gold drop.
- *The Marsh Ferry* — the Warden (the Ferryman) is here.
- *Gilman House, Room 428* — a secret room; the entrance to the Colour's Well chain.

**Lore beat:** the Innsmouth Look. If you have the Fisherman character, NPCs here don't attack you unprovoked.

---

### Floor 3 — The Restricted Archives

**Look:** Endless dark-wood stacks under green banker's lamps. Shelves that continue past where the ceiling should be. Card catalogues. Paper, dust, and the smell of old glue rendered as a palette: sepia, oxblood, brass.

**Mood:** *Bureaucratic dread.* Something here is organised, and you are a filing error.

**Mechanic — Shifting Shelves:** on a 15s cycle, bookshelf walls slide, changing cover and sightlines. Telegraphed by 2s of grinding audio and a floor-mounted brass rail glow. This forces adaptation mid-fight without being random.

**Signature rooms:**
- *The Card Catalogue* — hundreds of drawers; opening them costs nothing and randomly yields gold, a bullet trap, or a Codex fragment.
- *Reading Room 7* — no enemies. A desk, a lamp, a book. Reading it: +1 Corruption, a free Greater Inscription.
- *The Stacks Vertical* — a room the camera scrolls up through, all turrets.

**Lore beat:** this is where you learn what the Order actually wants, and that you were not the first to be sent.

---

### Floor 4 — The Mountains of Madness

**Look:** Whiteout blue, black basalt, five-pointed geometry everywhere. Cyclopean walls carved with the history of the Elder Things — murals the game actually renders as readable narrative if you stop to look.

**Mood:** *Deep time.* This place was ruined before there were people to ruin it.

**Mechanics:**
- **Cold** — a slow Sanity drain (1/s) outside of sheltered rooms and away from fire sources. Burning enemies and the Flare Pistol become genuinely valuable.
- **Blizzard zones** — vision radius clamped to 6 units.
- **Ice** — reduced friction in specific authored patches only. Never a whole room.

**Signature rooms:**
- *The Mural Hall* — no enemies, a long connector, the Elder Thing history in five panels. The game's only "walking simulator" moment, and it earns its keep.
- *The Dissection Chamber* — the Warden (*The Thing in the Ice*) is here, optional.
- *Camp Lake* — a supply camp; the shop, staffed by something wearing a parka.

**Lore beat:** Tekeli-li. The shoggoths learned to imitate their masters' voices. That's all they have left of them.

---

### Floor 5 — The Plateau of Leng

**Look:** No walls. A grey-violet plain under a sky with too many stars in the wrong arrangement. Monoliths. A distant, enormous, unlit city on the horizon that never gets closer. Fog.

**Mood:** *Agoraphobic.* After four floors of sealed rooms, having nowhere to hide is worse.

**Structure:** fully open, no room seals, no doors. Navigate by landmark and compass. See [06 §8](06-procedural-generation.md).

**Landmarks:**
- **Three Avatar Arenas** — the Black Pharaoh, the Bloated Woman, the Haunter of the Dark
- **The Caravan** — a merchant caravan of Men of Leng; the shop, and they are *polite*, which is the most unsettling thing in the game
- **Ruined Temples** ×2–3 — reward rooms, guarded
- **The Windowless Monastery** — optional, extremely dangerous, contains the entrance to Floor 7

**Lore beat:** here you meet an NPC who is you, from a previous run, and has been here a long time. They give you one true piece of information per run, tracked persistently.

---

### Floor 6 — R'lyeh

**Look:** Green-black stone, wet, at angles that hurt. Bioluminescence. Everything is monumentally, insultingly too large. Colour palette: deep viridian, bone, and a single wrong orange that appears only where Cthulhu's influence is strongest.

**Mood:** *Arrival.* Not fear — the terrible relief of having got where you were going.

**Mechanics:** seams (walk into a wall, exit elsewhere), volume violation (rooms bigger inside), and a lying minimap that only tells the truth when you're mad. See [06 §9](06-procedural-generation.md).

**Signature rooms:**
- *The Great Stair* — a staircase that returns you to where you started, three times, until you do something else.
- *The Drowned Colonnade* — the densest normal-enemy encounter in the game.
- *The Sepulchre* — the boss foyer. Absolutely silent.

## 4. Secret Content

### The Colour's Well (secret floor S)
**Access chain:** on Floor 2, find the secret room in Gilman House 428 → take the *Meteorite Fragment* → do not sell it → on Floor 3, place it in the Card Catalogue room's brass scale → a well appears on Floor 4.

Three rooms. Colourless, desaturating everything including your own UI. The Colour deals **permanent max-Sanity damage** instead of health damage — the only enemy in the game that does. Reward: **The Prism** (S-tier Cross sigil — your projectiles gain a random additional element and +40% damage, but your Sanity bar no longer displays a number).

### The Court of Azathoth (secret floor 7)
**Access:** enter the Windowless Monastery on Floor 5 at **Corruption ≥ 7**, having earned at least **three Unbroken Seals** this run. Both conditions gate on demonstrated skill, not repetition — a deliberate rejection of Gungeon's Bullet-That-Can-Kill-The-Past grind.

Five rooms. Pure bullet hell, no cover, no walls, no adds. This is where the game's density ceiling actually lives. Azathoth is not fought so much as *survived*: he does not know you exist, and the fight is about not waking him while the flutes play.

## 5. Endings

The game's ending is determined by the **state you arrive in**, not by a dialogue choice. This is the payoff for Corruption being a real strategic axis.

| Condition | Ending |
|---|---|
| Kill Cthulhu, Corruption ≤ 3 | **The Sealing.** You close the door. You go home. You are not the same, and the last shot is you not being able to look at the sea. |
| Kill Cthulhu, Corruption ≥ 7 | **The Inheritance.** You do not close the door. You take his place in the dreaming. The next run's Floor 6 has a new NPC: you. |
| Kill Cthulhu at Corruption 10 (Sovereign) | **The Yellow Ending.** It was never Cthulhu you were serving. The King in Yellow claims the win. Unlocks the Nameless character. |
| Survive Azathoth | **The True Ending.** The centre of the universe is an idiot god and the flute-players are the only thing holding reality together, and now you know it, and you have to go back up. |
| Die at any point | Run summary. The Order writes your name in the ledger. Literally — dead runs are listed by name in the Vestibule. |

**Persistent world-state:** each ending achieved permanently alters the Vestibule hub — new NPCs, new dialogue, the ledger grows. This is the cheapest possible long-tail retention mechanism and it costs almost nothing to build.

## 6. Lore Delivery Rules

1. **No cutscene exceeds 15 seconds.** Roguelike players restart constantly; anything longer becomes an obstacle.
2. **The Codex is the lore.** Every enemy, weapon, sigil, and boss gets an entry, unlocked on first encounter, with flavour text *and full mechanical detail*. Gungeon's Ammonomicon is the model, and it should be as good.
3. **Room dressing carries more lore than text.** The murals on Floor 4, the graffiti on Floor 1, the polite caravan on Floor 5.
4. **NPCs appear once per run at most** and never block movement.
5. **Never explain a monster fully.** The Codex entry for the Shoggoth should end mid-sentence.
6. **Public-domain sourcing.** Lovecraft's works and the Mythos entities used here (Cthulhu, Nyarlathotep, Azathoth, Dagon, Hydra, Yog-Sothoth, shoggoths, Deep Ones, ghouls, nightgaunts, the Elder Things, Leng, R'lyeh, Innsmouth, Arkham, Miskatonic) are public domain in the US and almost everywhere. **Avoid Derleth-era and later additions** (e.g. Cthugha, the elemental classification system) and avoid any Chaosium *Call of Cthulhu* RPG-specific terminology, artwork, or stat conventions — that material is under copyright. Write our own creature names where in doubt.
